# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is the app's owner, with a small circle of friends and family who also use it casually. It's not a public product — a small, known group identifying plants from their phones, usually in the moment while out and about.

## Product Purpose

Plant Tracker exists to make identifying a plant from a photo fast and frictionless, and to quietly build a **growing personal record** of everything that's been identified over time. The core loop is capture → identify → save, repeated casually whenever someone in the group encounters a plant worth knowing about. Success is a confident answer in seconds, and a history that becomes more valuable to look back through the longer it's used.

## Positioning

Unlike a generic plant-ID app, the point isn't just the identification — it's that every photo and result gets saved, building a personal, browsable log of plants discovered over time. The identification is the hook; the growing record is the reason to keep using it.

## Brand Personality

Calm, natural, clean, and warm — like a well-kept field notebook, not a tech product. Minimal and unfussy, but never cold. Personality should come through in small human touches (copy, motion, imagery), not decoration.

## Anti-references

Should not read as a corporate SaaS dashboard: no boxy card grids treated as the default layout, no sidebar nav dense with stat tiles, no enterprise-tool coldness. This is a personal tool for a small group, not an admin panel.

## Design Principles

- **Get out of the way of the moment.** The camera → identify → result loop is the most frequent action; minimize taps, loading friction, and visual noise on that path.
- **The record is the reward.** History/browsing deserves as much design care as capture — it's the part that makes the app worth returning to over months and years, not an afterthought list view.
- **Calm and human, never corporate.** Favor warmth and natural texture over polish-for-its-own-sake; avoid dashboard/enterprise conventions (dense grids, stat tiles, sidebar chrome).
- **Small circle, not anonymous public.** A few known people use this together — it can afford a little personality and informality that a public-facing product couldn't.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard good practice: solid color contrast, reasonably sized tap targets for one-handed phone use, no strict WCAG compliance level required given the small, known user base.
